# The Single Income Stress Test: five takeaways

Yesterday we rebuilt our [Single Income Stress Test](https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/stress-test-viz), a map that takes one person's annual pay, subtracts the local poverty threshold for their family, and subtracts money for surprise expenses, for every U.S. metro and rural area from 2015 to 2025. Here are five things it shows. Unless we say otherwise, the figures are for 2025, a median earner, and two adults and two children who rent. The test uses one job's pay before taxes, so read it as an illustration, not a poverty rate.

## 1. The result turns on about $10,000

The chart below shows the share of U.S. jobs in areas where one median paycheck falls short, as the money set aside for surprise expenses goes from $0 to $25,000.

![Line chart titled The result turns on about $10,000. The share of U.S. jobs in areas where one median paycheck falls short of the local poverty threshold plus money for surprise expenses, 2025, rises from 0.6% with nothing set aside to 15% at $5,000, 49% at $10,000, 87% at $15,000 and 96% at $20,000. At $10,000, one income falls short in 283 of 521 areas.](images/01-cushion-curve.png)

With nothing set aside, one income falls short in 5 of 521 areas. At $10,000 it falls short in 283, and at $20,000 in 515. The line climbs fastest near $10,000 because, in the typical area, the median paycheck clears the local poverty threshold by $9,694.

::: spacer

## 2. Below the median, there is no cushion to speak of

The median is the middle of the pay scale. The chart below runs the same test at five pay levels, with nothing set aside for surprises.

![Bar chart titled Below the median, there is no cushion to speak of. Share of U.S. jobs in areas where pay is below the local poverty threshold for two adults and two children, with nothing set aside, 2025: 100% at the 10th percentile (521 of 521 areas), 91.7% at the 25th (418 areas), 0.6% at the median (5 areas), and 0% at the 75th and 90th.](images/02-pay-levels-no-cushion.png)

At the 25th percentile, pay is below the local poverty threshold in 418 of 521 areas, which hold 91.7% of the jobs. At the 10th percentile, it is below the threshold in all 521.

San Jose shows why the median alone is not enough. The chart below sets pay at five levels in the San Jose metro area against its poverty threshold, the highest on the map.

![Bar chart titled San Jose: room at the median, none a step below. Annual pay in the San Jose metro area in 2025 against the local poverty threshold of $61,763: $41,590 at the 10th percentile and $49,800 at the 25th, both below the threshold, and $84,050 at the median, $166,940 at the 75th and $224,240 at the 90th, all above it. The 25th percentile is $11,963 short; the median clears it by $22,287.](images/03-san-jose-pay-scale.png)

San Jose's median wage of $84,050 clears the threshold by $22,287. Its 25th percentile wage of $49,800 falls $11,963 short. That drop from the median to the 25th percentile, 41%, is the largest of the 387 metro areas. In the typical metro area it is 25%.

San Jose is not alone. Of the 238 areas that pass the $10,000 test at the median, 135 are below the threshold at the 25th percentile with nothing set aside.

::: spacer

## 3. The big metros that fall short are all in the South and West

The chart below ranks the 36 metro areas with 1,000,000 or more jobs by the result of the test with a $10,000 cushion.

![Bar chart titled Big metros that fall short: all South and West. One median paycheck, less the local poverty threshold, less $10,000, in the 36 metro areas with 1,000,000 or more jobs, 2025. 15 fall short, led by Miami ($9,545 short), Orlando, Riverside, Las Vegas and Los Angeles. The most room is in San Jose ($12,287), Seattle and Washington, DC. Midwest and Northeast metros such as Chicago, Detroit, New York and Boston have room.](images/04-largest-metros-2025.png)

One income falls short in 15 of the 36. All 15 are in California, Florida, Texas, Arizona, Nevada, Georgia, Tennessee and North Carolina. None is in the Midwest or the Northeast. Texas, California and Florida together hold 48% of all the jobs in areas that fall short.

::: spacer

## 4. The poverty threshold rose faster than pay

The chart below follows the national poverty threshold and the median wage in the typical area since 2015.

![Line chart titled The poverty threshold rose faster than pay. From 2015 to 2025 the national poverty threshold for two adults and two children who rent rose 63.0%, and the median wage in the typical area rose 45.8%. The two lines stay close through 2021, then the threshold pulls away in 2022 and 2023.](images/05-threshold-vs-pay.png)

The threshold rose 63.0% in ten years. The median wage in the typical area rose 45.8%. Most of the gap opened in two years: the threshold rose 9.7% in 2022 and 8.6% in 2023, while the typical area's median wage rose 4.7% and 5.8%. The threshold follows what families spend on food, clothing, shelter and utilities, so it can rise faster than prices. In 2020 the Census Bureau also changed how much of it moves with local rents.

The national figures for the same ten years read differently. The chart below puts two of them beside ours.

![Three pairs of bars titled Two readings of the same ten years, 2015 and 2025. The official poverty rate fell from 13.5% to 10.2%. Median household income in 2025 dollars rose from $74,590 to $87,460. The median wage as a percent of the local poverty threshold, in the typical area of 290, fell from 139% to 125%.](images/06-two-readings.png)

The official poverty rate fell from 13.5% to 10.2%. Median household income, adjusted for inflation, rose 17.3%. Over the same years, the median wage in the typical area went from 139% of the local poverty threshold to 125%. It fell in 285 of the 290 areas we can compare.

These measure different things. Household income counts every earner in a home and is adjusted for prices. Our figure is one job's pay against a threshold that follows what families spend on basics.

::: spacer

## 5. Every large metro we can compare lost ground

The chart below shows the same measure, the median wage as a percent of the local poverty threshold, for large metro areas in 2015 and 2025.

![Bar chart titled Every large metro we can compare lost ground. Median wage as a percent of the local poverty threshold, 2015 and 2025, in 30 metro areas with 1,000,000 or more jobs and stable boundaries. It fell in all 30: Charlotte from 149% to 119%, Houston from 147% to 117%, San Jose from 164% to 136%. Charlotte, Houston, Phoenix, Atlanta, Dallas and Nashville went from clearing the $10,000 test to falling short.](images/07-large-metros-since-2015.png)

It fell in all 30. The largest drops were in Charlotte (149% to 119%), Houston (147% to 117%) and San Jose (164% to 136%). The smallest were in Washington, DC (152% to 141%) and Baltimore (142% to 131%).

On the test with a $10,000 cushion, 21 of the 30 had room in 2015 and 15 did in 2025. Charlotte, Houston, Phoenix, Atlanta, Dallas and Nashville went from having room to falling short. None went the other way.

The chart covers large metro areas whose boundaries did not change, or changed by less than 1% of their jobs. That leaves out Chicago, Indianapolis, New York and Pittsburgh. Boston and Cleveland are left out because their area codes changed in 2024.

::: blurb What this does and does not tell you
- One job's pay before taxes, from the BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics survey, May 2015 to May 2025. It leaves out a second earner, tax credits and aid, and it does not subtract taxes, child care or medical bills.
- The threshold is the Census Bureau's Supplemental Poverty Measure threshold, which is higher where rents are higher. For 112 smaller metro areas in 2025 it is our estimate from local rents.
- A shortfall does not mean a family is in poverty. It means one paycheck does not cover the threshold and the cushion together.
- Dollars are not adjusted for inflation. Comparisons across years use pay as a percent of the threshold, with no cushion.
- The full method is on the [map's page](https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/stress-test-viz).
:::

## Common questions

### Can a family live on one income?

By this test, in 2025 the median paycheck covers the local poverty threshold for two adults and two children and a $10,000 cushion in 238 of 521 areas. At the 25th percentile of pay, with no cushion at all, it covers the threshold in 103.

### Which big cities are hardest to live in on one income?

Among metro areas with 1,000,000 or more jobs, the largest shortfalls with a $10,000 cushion are in Miami, Orlando, Riverside, Las Vegas and Los Angeles. The most room is in San Jose, Seattle and Washington, DC.

### Is it getting harder to live on one income?

From 2015 to 2025, the median wage fell as a percent of the local poverty threshold in 285 of the 290 areas we can compare, from 139% to 125% in the typical area.

### Why does this differ from the official poverty rate?

They measure different things. The official rate fell from 13.5% in 2015 to 10.2% in 2025, using a line that is the same everywhere and rises only with prices. This test sets one job's pay against a local threshold that follows what families spend on basics.

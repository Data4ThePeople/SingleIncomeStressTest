## LinkedIn

Media: the cushion GIF, or the hero and `images/02-viz-screenshot-email.jpg`, or the tutorial video (`video/single-income-stress-test-tutorial.mp4`, 57 seconds). LinkedIn takes one kind of media per post.

```
Can a family live on one income where you live?

Back in January we published the Single Income Stress Test, a simple "simulation." Take one person's annual pay. Subtract the local poverty threshold for their family. Subtract some money for surprise expenses (think, a hospital bill or a busted car that needs fixing). What is left is the excess, or the shortfall.

Yesterday, we rebuilt it from scratch in Python. It now shows every year from 2015 to 2025, covers rural areas as well as metro areas, and lets you change the family size, the housing and the surprise expenses.

The entire purpose of this analysis is to understand the impact of surprise expenses. With no cushion (the on-paper world, where the government's poverty threshold leaves no room for surprises), the median paycheck falls short of the threshold in just five areas in America. No problem here! With $10,000, one income falls short in 283 of 521 areas, and those areas hold 49% of the jobs. With $20,000, it falls short in 515. That's what folks call "precarity." This visualization quantifies it for where you live.

One limit, up front: this is an illustration (or simulation), not a poverty rate. It uses one job's pay before taxes, and it leaves out a second earner, tax credits and aid.

One finding from the 11 years stood out to us. Among the 290 areas we can compare, the median wage rose faster than the local poverty threshold in only five areas. More takeaways coming tomorrow.

Find your city and see which way it went: https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/stress-test-viz
```
